About Darjeeling

Colonial charm, world-famous tea and front-row views of Kanchenjunga.

Darjeeling sits on a ridge at just over 2,000 metres, surrounded by the tea gardens that made its name famous around the world. On a clear morning the entire Kanchenjunga range fills the northern sky.

Ride the Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, watch the sun rise from Tiger Hill, sip first-flush tea where it is grown and wander the Mall and Chowrasta as the evening mist rolls in.

Good to know

Questions about Darjeeling

A quick overview of our most-asked questions. Still unsure? Ask us on WhatsApp.

01When can I see Kanchenjunga clearly?

October to December offers the most reliable clear skies. Spring (March to May) is pleasant but often hazy by mid-morning, so plan for early starts.

02Is the toy train ride worth it?

Absolutely. The short joyride from Darjeeling to Ghoom and back, including a stop at the Batasia Loop, takes about two hours and is the easiest way to experience this UNESCO-listed railway.

03Can I visit a tea estate?

Yes. Several estates offer guided walks and tastings, and some have heritage bungalows where you can stay overnight.

04How many days do I need in Darjeeling?

Two to three nights is ideal. Darjeeling pairs well with Gangtok, which is about 4 hours away by road.

05What should I pack?

Layers. Even in summer, mornings and evenings are cool, and Tiger Hill before dawn can be close to freezing.
