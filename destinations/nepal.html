About Nepal

UNESCO heritage in Kathmandu, lakeside calm in Pokhara and the world's highest mountains.

Nepal packs the world's highest mountains, seven UNESCO World Heritage Sites in the Kathmandu Valley alone, and a deeply spiritual culture into one small country. Kathmandu, Patan and Bhaktapur are living museums of temples, courtyards and craftsmanship.

A short flight or scenic drive away, Pokhara sits beside the calm Phewa Lake with the Annapurna range and the fishtail peak of Machapuchare rising behind it - the perfect base for sunrise viewpoints, paragliding and gentle treks.

Good to know

Questions about Nepal

A quick overview of our most-asked questions. Still unsure? Ask us on WhatsApp.

01Do I need a visa for Nepal?

Indian nationals do not need a visa and can travel with a passport or voter ID. Most other nationalities can get a visa on arrival at Kathmandu airport or land borders.

02When is the best time to visit Nepal?

October and November offer the clearest mountain views and festival season. March to May is warm, with rhododendrons in bloom. June to September is monsoon.

03Can I see Mount Everest?

Yes - take an early-morning mountain flight from Kathmandu for close views of Everest and the Khumbu range, without trekking.

04Is Nepal suitable for non-trekkers?

Completely. Our Nepal trips focus on culture, food, viewpoints and comfortable hotels. Short hikes are optional.

05What currency is used in Nepal?

The Nepalese rupee. Indian rupee notes of 100 and below are widely accepted, and cards work in most hotels and larger shops.
